# Game Presentation

Use when creating or polishing a game. Follow the requested style and existing visual language; choose techniques for the scene's needs.

## Scene

- Give the player, hazards, and objectives distinct silhouettes or contrast at the actual display size. Assign palette colors consistent roles; a limited palette or plain background can be intentional.
- Keep HUD text readable against the scene and clear of important action. A shadow, border, or reserved area can help when contrast alone is insufficient.
- Match sprite detail and scale to the game. When sprite art is requested, make recognizable forms rather than treating a colored rectangle as a finished character.

## Feedback

- Make consequential actions and outcomes perceptible: for example, a changed tile, hit animation, score update, or sound cue. Feedback should clarify what happened without obscuring play.
- Choose animation, particles, screen shake, scrolling, and audio only where they serve the requested experience. Check timing in motion when a still frame cannot show it.
- When combining effects and music, allocate channels deliberately so a cue does not unexpectedly interrupt another sound. Judge balance by listening.

## Flow

- Make controls and objectives discoverable when the player needs them.
- Make success, failure, and recovery clear when those states exist; avoid unnecessary steps before play or retry.
