# Case study: a full adversarial read of a finished novel

This is a real, verifiable worked example of the skill run against a complete manuscript - the
Hollow House Films novel *The Saga for Donna* (19 chapters). Every quote and outcome below is drawn
from the book's own append-only process ledgers, written as the work happened, not reconstructed. It
exists to show, concretely, what the room does when it reads a novel: it finds defects, cuts or flags
them, and hands every creative call back to the author. The read itself composed nothing - every fix
was a subtraction.

One honest note up front, because this book makes the point better than a clean example would: parts
of this novel had earlier been *expanded* with AI help, strictly from the author's own material. That
is a different activity from the adversarial read, and the read's most striking result is that it
caught the expansion's own mistakes. Disclosed plainly here, because that transparency is the whole
ethic of the thing.

Spoiler note: this stays at craft level and does not reveal the ending or any character's fate.

## What was run

Each unit - one chapter, never the whole book at once - was read twice: once at reading speed as a
first-time reader, then again logging every stumble by type, quoting the exact line. Then a separate
continuity pass traced recurring facts across the whole book. All 19 chapters were read this way.

## The headline

The single most important result is not a defect. It's this line from the ledger, after eleven
chapters:

> "Seven of the fourteen defects fixed so far were introduced by the expansion pass. Every one of
> them was invisible to the automated detector and visible on a single cold read."

Half the defects were the machine's *own* earlier expansion mistakes, caught only by the machine
reading itself cold, as a stranger. The ledger's own conclusion became the argument for reading this
way at all: **"Generated prose needs the reader pass more than written prose does, not less."** AI
assistance does not remove the need for a hostile read. It creates it.

## The seven stumbles, in the wild

**CONTRADICTION - a route that can't be walked (Ch One).** The prose put "the stairs were gone, a
wall of orange where the down should be" and then sent the character down anyway, past the kitchen,
into the basement. Impossible as written. Fixed by restoring the book's own established geography - the
way out is blocked at the front - so the movement stops contradicting itself. Nothing added.

**STOPPED - a pronoun reaching the wrong way (Ch Two, Four, Eleven, and more).** "She knocked at
407" opened a paragraph whose nearest antecedent was the wrong woman; fixed to "The caseworker
knocked at 407." One word. This exact failure recurred, so the ledger stopped fixing them one at a
time and flagged the class for a single mechanical sweep - "worth one mechanical sweep of every
paragraph-opening pronoun in the book rather than catching them one chapter at a time." It ended up
"the sixth wrong-antecedent pronoun found" across the whole read.

**CONTRADICTION - a physical fact done twice (Ch Three, Eight).** A pie dish "washed by the sink"
early is "washed" again days later; the fix cut the second washing rather than rewrite. Elsewhere a
hundred-dollar bill goes into a pocket twice in one sentence; the figure stayed, the literal repeat
was cut.

**CONTRADICTION - the clock says two things (Ch Three, Five).** "Downtown Akron lay hazed in the late
light" sat inside a scene the chapter calls a Saturday morning; restored to the scene's own morning
framing, a one-word change. In Ch Five, "the first real quiet he'd had all night" ended a scene that
opened at sunset - the night hadn't happened. Cut the clause.

**LOST - a plant that moves (continuity pass).** A neighbor lives "two doors down in 405" on the
fortieth floor in one chapter and "the thirty-third" in two others - load-bearing, because that floor
is where a recurring object and the fire both land. Two chapters place her on forty; the number that
broke it was dropped to restore the book's own consistency, not chosen by preference.

## The two defects the room refused to fix, and escalated

The room's job on a structural problem is to surface it, not to solve it. Two of the biggest findings
were declined on purpose and handed to the author:

**The double waking (Ch Six into Seven).** One morning is depicted as two wakings - two different
people waking first, two different partings. The contradiction ran too deep to close with a clean cut,
and every available fix would have meant cutting a chapter's protected ending or reordering two
chapters on the machine's own judgment - forbidden. "Declined and escalated." It was later closed *on
the author's instruction*, and only "by relocating existing text rather than composing any."

**The deadline that fails its own arithmetic (Ch Thirteen into Fourteen).** A character's plan
requires a building gone before a Monday inspection, yet four speeches later he sets the deadline as
"next Saturday" - six days the wrong side of Monday. "By his own plan the burn must precede Monday, so
the deadline he hands over is wrong by his own reasoning." Load-bearing, and closing it would touch a
protected chapter ending. Declined and escalated with a proposed direction, for the author to rule on.

Both are the model outcome: found, quoted, proven against the page, and left for the human - **the
room proposes, the author disposes.**

## Verify before you touch - the false-positive discipline

Roughly a third of every flag is a false alarm. Each claimed defect is proven against the page before
anything is touched, and the ones that dissolve on a second look are named so a later pass won't
re-raise them:

> "A 'she' in a two-character scene that actually resolves, a present-tense line that is correctly
> gnomic, a compound hyphen that is not a broken dash, a name that appears once on purpose."

All flagged by a detector, all verified as fine, all left exactly as the author wrote them. The
governing rule: **"Changing a false positive is worse than missing a real one."**

## What it refused to touch

Protected conventions were declared off-limits and stayed that way, recorded as choices with reasons:

- A 110-word speech that "carries the whole conspiracy" was left long *by choice* - "closing it needs
  an invented reaction beat (forbidden), or cutting from the scene the story turns on. Metric stays
  open at 1 by choice."
- The foreknowledge / elegy voice was left as CHOICE every time a detector tripped on it.
- The endings were never touched.

The ledger's principle: **"A permanently open gap with a stated reason is a correct outcome."**

## Every fix was a subtraction

The through-line: fixes removed or restored, never added. A pass tightening over-long paragraphs was "verified by
token-stream diff that exactly 55 words left and nothing was reordered or orphaned." The one narrative
"we" in the entire novel - "We still didn't see his face. Not yet.", an out-of-register intrusion that
read like a stage instruction - was matched to the book's own existing prose, "No face yet."

## The honest bottom line

A finished novel was read twice end to end, its contradictions and stumbles found and quoted, a third
of the flags correctly thrown out, its deliberate conventions protected, its structural problems handed
back to the author, and its line-level defects closed by cutting or by restoring the book's own
consistency. Where AI had earlier helped expand the prose, the read is what caught that expansion's
own slips. The book came out tighter and truer to itself. The story - every event, character, and line
of dialogue - is the author's, and the read added not one word to it. It only ever subtracted.
