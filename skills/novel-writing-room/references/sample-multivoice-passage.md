# Demonstration fixture: two voices, one break

This is a public-domain excerpt in two voices (Bram Stoker, *Dracula*, 1897: Jonathan Harker's
journal, then Mina Murray's letter) into which **two defects have been deliberately injected** - they
are not Stoker - plus **one famous trap** that is genuine to the book's own conventions. It exists to
demonstrate the per-voice rules: calibrate each voice separately, flag a voice only for breaking its
own norm, never for differing from another's - and track which narrator knows what.

## The passage

### Jonathan Harker's Journal

*3 May. Bistritz.* - Left Munich at 8:35 P. M., on 1st May, arriving at Vienna early next morning;
should have arrived at 6:46, but train was an hour late. Buda-Pesth seems a wonderful place, from the
glimpse which I got of it from the train and the little I could walk through the streets. I feared to
go very far from the station, as we had arrived late and would start as near the correct time as
possible. The impression I had was that we were leaving the West and entering the East; the most
western of splendid bridges over the Danube, which is here of noble width and depth, took us among
the traditions of Turkish rule.

We left in pretty good time, and came after nightfall to Klausenburgh. Here I stopped for the night
at the Hotel Royale. I had for dinner, or rather supper, a chicken done up some way with red pepper,
which was very good but thirsty. The paprika was amazing, ten out of ten. (*Mem.*, get recipe for
Mina.) I asked the waiter, and he said it was called "paprika hendl," and that, as it was a national
dish, I should be able to get it anywhere along the Carpathians.

### Letter, Mina Murray to Lucy Westenra

"My dearest Lucy,--

"Forgive my long delay in writing, but I have been simply overwhelmed with work. The life of an
assistant schoolmistress is sometimes trying. I am longing to be with you, and by the sea, where we
can talk together freely and build our castles in the air. I have been working very hard lately,
because I want to keep up with Jonathan's studies, and I have been practising shorthand very
assiduously. When we are married I shall be able to be useful to Jonathan. He wrote to me of the
grey castle above the pass, though his letter has not yet arrived. He and I sometimes write letters
in shorthand, and he is keeping a stenographic journal of his travels abroad. When I am with you I
shall keep a diary in the same way.

## Answer key (do not read until your report is written)

**Calibration first, or everything below goes wrong.** Two voices: Harker, an 1897 solicitor's
travel diary - dated entries, clipped observations, (*Mem.*) notes-to-self, formal register. Mina, a
warm, running, affectionate letter voice. They are *supposed* to differ. The difference between them
is not a finding, and a report that flags Mina for not sounding like Harker, or the letter for not
being a diary, has failed the per-voice rule.

**[V1] STOPPED (register break) - Harker, para 2.** "The paprika was amazing, ten out of ten."
Injected: a modern casual register inside an 1897 diary voice that has just said "very good but
thirsty" in its own idiom. The voice breaks *its own* norm - that is the only kind of voice finding
the room is allowed. Fix: cut the sentence; the authentic appraisal is already there.

**[V2] CONTRADICTION (frame knowledge) - Mina's letter.** "He wrote to me of the grey castle above
the pass, though his letter has not yet arrived." Injected: the sentence contradicts itself at the
frame level - she reports the contents of a letter she says she has not received. This is "a
character knowing what they were never told," the failure that hides at narrative borders, caught
only if the ledger tracks which narrator holds which fact. Fix: flag for author; within this
excerpt, cutting the clause about the castle clears it.

**[FP] The trap - Harker's entry header, and it is genuine to the book.** "*3 May. Bistritz.*" heads
an entry that narrates the 1st of May onward: a multi-day journey under a single date. A naive pass
flags a dating error. The disproof: diary convention - the header is the date and place of writing,
recounting the days just travelled. The book's own frame explains it. TRIAGE: CHOICE.

**Scoring a demonstration run:** full marks is both injected defects found with anchors and disproof
lines, the dating trap protected as CHOICE with the convention named, and the difference between the
two voices left entirely alone.
