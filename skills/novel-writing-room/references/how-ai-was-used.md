# How AI Was Used - a reusable, honest statement

A fill-in-the-blanks template for any author who wants to tell readers, plainly and without
overstating, how AI was used on their book. It describes **adversarial** use only - the machine reading
a finished manuscript to find its flaws, and fixing them by cutting or by restoring the book's own
consistency, never by composing new prose.

**Use it only if that is the whole truth.** If AI also helped you draft, expand, or generate any of the
prose, plot, characters, or dialogue, this statement would be false as written. That kind of help can
be perfectly legitimate, but it is a different thing and it must be disclosed as itself - see "If AI
also helped you write" at the bottom. Do not use the clean adversarial-only claim to paper over
drafting help.

Replace the bracketed parts. Delete any line that isn't true of your process. Keep it plain.

## The short answer (paste-ready, adversarial-only)

> **Did AI write the book?**
> No. [AUTHOR] wrote it. AI was the adversary, not the author - a tireless proofreader and continuity
> checker pointed at the finished manuscript with one job: to break it, so a human could make it
> stronger. It invented no plot, no characters, no dialogue, and no events, and it composed no prose of
> its own. It found defects, checked each against the page, fixed line-level mistakes by cutting or by
> restoring the book's own consistency, and flagged everything structural for [AUTHOR] to decide. The
> words are the author's. The scrutiny was the machine's.

## The core distinction (lead with this whenever the topic comes up)

- **Generative use** would be "write me a chapter," "invent a character," "give me a plot." None of
  that happened.
- **Adversarial use** is the opposite: the manuscript already existed, and AI was pointed at it like
  a hostile editor whose only job was to break it - to hunt for continuity errors, contradictions,
  weak seams, and mechanical mistakes, and report them so a human could decide what to fix.
- The book is human-authored. AI invented no event, line of dialogue, character, or outcome.

## How the process worked

1. **The manuscript is the only source.** AI worked from the novel itself and nothing else - no
   outline, no script, no notes. When the prose ran thin, the answer was never to add words; it was to
   cut, tighten, or hand the gap back to the author.
2. **Adversarial review.** The manuscript was read with instructions to find defects, not to compliment
   it - broken continuity, misattributed dialogue, places where the prose explains what the scene
   already showed, punctuation damage, and structural mistakes.
3. **Verify before touching anything.** Every flaw AI claimed was checked against the actual text first.
   Many "problems" were false alarms and were left alone. A claim that couldn't be proven against the
   page was discarded.
4. **Fix by cutting, never inventing.** When a real defect was confirmed, the fix was a cut, or a
   restoration of the book's own established facts - the smallest change that removed the stumble. If a
   fix couldn't point back to something already on the page, it did not go in.
5. **A full reader pass.** AI read the book start to finish at reading speed, simulating a first-time
   reader, and flagged every spot where a reader might stumble. Those were sorted into real defects
   versus matters of taste, and only the defects were addressed. Taste and voice stayed the author's.

## Guardrails when answering questions about it

- Never imply AI wrote or co-wrote the book. It reviewed and stress-tested a finished, human-authored
  manuscript.
- Don't overstate. If asked something not covered here, say AI's role was limited to adversarial review
  and proofreading, and that the author made every creative and structural decision.
- Don't reveal plot spoilers, endings, or who-did-what when giving examples.
- Keep the tone plain and confident. This was quality control - the kind an author gets from a tough
  human editor, done faster and more thoroughly.

## If AI also helped you write (a separate, honest disclosure)

If AI helped draft or expand any prose - even strictly from your own outline or script, even with a
no-invention rule - then the adversarial-only statement above is not the whole truth, and using it
alone would mislead. Say the fuller thing plainly, for example:

> The story is entirely [AUTHOR]'s - every event, character, and line of dialogue. AI helped render
> [AUTHOR]'s own [material] into prose under a strict rule against inventing anything new, and then was
> turned on the result adversarially to cut what didn't work. No plot, character, or dialogue was
> invented by AI.

Honest and specific beats clean and overstated. Readers forgive disclosed AI assistance far more
easily than a purity claim that turns out to be too good to be true.
