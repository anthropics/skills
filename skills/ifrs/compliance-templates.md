# IFRS Compliance Templates

Use these templates when producing structured compliance deliverables. Each template includes header fields, a tabular body, and summary sections. Adapt column widths and detail to the engagement scope.

---

## 1. Disclosure Completeness Checklist

### Purpose

Systematically verify that all mandatory and encouraged disclosures required by a specific IFRS standard have been addressed in the financial statements. This is the primary tool for disclosure compliance reviews.

### Header Fields

| Field | Value |
|---|---|
| **Standard** | [e.g., IFRS 16 Leases] |
| **Entity** | [Legal entity name] |
| **Reporting Period** | [e.g., Year ended 31 December 2025] |
| **Prepared by** | [Name, title] |
| **Date of Review** | [DD/MM/YYYY] |
| **Reviewed by** | [Name, title] |
| **Review Date** | [DD/MM/YYYY] |

### Checklist Table

Draw the rows from the per-standard library in §1A below, which carries a verified paragraph reference against every requirement. Copy the table for the standard under review, delete the rows that are not applicable to the entity (rather than marking them N/A, if the checklist is being used as a working paper rather than as evidence of completeness), and add a Notes column:

| # | Disclosure Requirement | Paragraph Ref | Included (Yes / No / N/A) | Notes |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | [from §1A] | [from §1A] | [ ] | [Cross-reference to financial statement page/note] |

Two rules that decide most disclosure arguments before they start:

- **A requirement is not satisfied by a policy sentence.** Most of the lines in §1A ask for an amount, a reconciliation or a maturity band. A narrative paragraph describing the policy does not discharge a requirement to disclose the amount.
- **Immaterial disclosures are not merely permitted omissions -- including them is a defect.** IFRS Practice Statement 2 treats obscuring material information with immaterial information as a failure in its own right (IFRS Practice Statement 2.57). Run the materiality template in §6 over a completed checklist before concluding that every "No" is a gap.

**Key Gaps Identified**

| # | Disclosure Requirement | Paragraph Ref | Impact / Risk | Recommended Action |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Variable lease payments not included in lease liabilities | IFRS 16.53(e) | Medium -- incomplete picture of lease costs; potential audit finding | Quantify variable lease payments by class and disclose in Note 12 |
| 2 | Future cash outflows not reflected in lease liabilities, including exposure to variable lease payments | IFRS 16.59(b), IFRS 16.59(b)(i), IFRS 16.B49 | Medium -- users cannot assess exposure to variable rents or understand the basis of the variability | Add the nature and basis of the variability (revenue-linked rents) and a sensitivity or range disclosure |

**Reference corrections applied to this example.** Eight paragraph references in an earlier version of this worked example cited paragraphs that exist but deal with something else, and one row duplicated another. All were re-checked against the text of IFRS 16 and corrected above. The recurring traps: IFRS 16.51 is the disclosure *objective*, not the presentation requirement (that is 47(a)); IFRS 16.52 is the single-note requirement, not the lease liability presentation requirement (that is 47(b)); IFRS 16.55 is the short-term lease *commitment* disclosure, not a general "additional information" catch-all (that is 59); IFRS 16.C12(a) is the weighted average IBR and C12(b) is the IAS 17 commitments reconciliation; IFRS 16.56 and 16.57 concern right-of-use assets meeting the investment property definition and revalued right-of-use assets, not variable lease payments; and IFRS 16 has no general judgement-disclosure requirement at all.

## 1A. Per-Standard Disclosure Checklist Library

### Purpose

A verified, paragraph-referenced checklist for every standard in `standards-reference.md`. Copy
the relevant table into the §1 template. Every reference has been checked against the paragraph
text of the standard; where a lettered sub-item is cited, that sub-item exists in that paragraph.

### How to use it

1. Identify the standards in scope from the entity's balance sheet and the trial balance, not
   from last year's checklist. A standard becomes relevant the first time a transaction occurs.
2. Work the table top to bottom. Mark `N/A` only where the underlying transaction or balance
   does not exist — not where the amount is small. Size is a materiality question (§6), not an
   applicability question.
3. Any line marked `No` goes to the Key Gaps table in §1 with an impact assessment.

### Which of IAS 1 / IFRS 18 applies

| Annual period beginning | Presentation standard | Going concern | Accounting policy disclosures | Judgements and estimation uncertainty |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Before 1 Jan 2027 (unless early-adopting IFRS 18) | IAS 1 | IAS 1.25-26 | IAS 1.117-117E | IAS 1.122-133 |
| On or after 1 Jan 2027 | IFRS 18 | IAS 8.6K-6L | IAS 8.27A-27E | IAS 8.27G, IAS 8.31A-31I |

IFRS 18 does not carry the going-concern requirement. IFRS 18 moved it, unchanged, into IAS 8,
and IAS 8 was retitled *Basis of Preparation of Financial Statements* to reflect that it now
holds fair presentation, compliance, going concern, the accrual basis and accounting-policy
disclosure. Preparers looking for going concern in IFRS 18 will not find it there.

### Known gap — IFRS 20

**There is no IFRS 20 checklist in this library, and its absence is a known gap rather than an oversight.** IFRS 20 *Regulatory Assets and Regulatory Liabilities* was issued on 27 May 2026, effective for annual periods beginning on or after 1 January 2029 with earlier application permitted, and is not yet EU-endorsed. It was issued after the annual edition of the Standards from which every table below was built closed, so its paragraph-numbered text was not available to verify against and no table could be written to the evidential standard the rest of this library meets. IFRS 20 supersedes **IFRS 14 *Regulatory Deferral Accounts***, which is covered below and remains the applicable Standard until an entity applies IFRS 20. Anyone adding the IFRS 20 table will need a source outside that edition — the Standard's own landing page or the issued PDF.

No other standard in `standards-reference.md` is missing from this library.

### Worked Example: Revenue Recognition for Multi-Element Software Arrangement (IFRS 15)

```
TECHNICAL ACCOUNTING MEMORANDUM

Entity:          NovaTech Solutions Inc.
Prepared by:     A. Morales, Senior Manager
Date:            05/03/2026
Reviewed by:     K. Lindgren, Partner
Review Date:     12/03/2026
Reference:       NT-2025-REV-003

---------------------------------------------------------------

1. ISSUE

NovaTech entered into a CU 2,400,000 contract with Meridian
Financial Services on 1 July 2025 for (a) a perpetual software
license, (b) implementation and customisation services, and
(c) three years of post-contract support (PCS) including
unspecified software updates and telephone/online support.

The question is how to identify the performance obligations,
allocate the transaction price, and determine the timing of
revenue recognition under IFRS 15.

2. APPLICABLE STANDARDS

- IFRS 15 Revenue from Contracts with Customers
  (paragraphs 22-30: identifying performance obligations;
   paragraphs 47-72: allocating the transaction price;
   paragraphs 31-45: determining the transaction price;
   paragraphs 35-38: variable consideration;
   paragraphs 73-90: satisfying performance obligations)
- IFRS 15.B34-B41: Licenses of intellectual property
- IFRS 15.IE -- Illustrative Examples (Examples 54-62:
  licensing, combined goods and services)

3. FACTS AND CIRCUMSTANCES

Contract details:

- Customer: Meridian Financial Services (large regional bank)
- Contract date: 1 July 2025
- Total stated price: CU 2,400,000
  - Perpetual license fee: CU 1,200,000 (payable on delivery)
  - Implementation/customisation: CU 600,000 (payable in three
    milestones over 6 months)
  - PCS: CU 200,000 per annum for 3 years (CU 600,000 total,
    payable annually in advance)
- The perpetual license grants Meridian the right to use
  NovaTech's core banking analytics platform (version 8.2).
- Implementation involves configuration, data migration, and
  customisation of standard reports. Estimated duration is
  6 months (July 2025 -- December 2025). The customisation
  modifies existing report templates but does not alter the
  underlying software code.
- PCS includes (i) unspecified software updates ("when-and-if
  available") and (ii) telephone and online technical support
  during business hours.
- NovaTech regularly sells the perpetual license on a standalone
  basis for CU 1,000,000 to CU 1,300,000 (median CU 1,150,000).
- NovaTech sells implementation services for comparable-scope
  projects at CU 500,000 to CU 700,000 (median CU 600,000).
- NovaTech sells PCS renewals at CU 180,000 to CU 220,000 per
  annum (median CU 200,000 per annum; CU 600,000 for 3 years).
- No significant financing component (all payments within
  12 months of the related performance).
- No variable consideration, rights of return, or penalties.
- The software is functional without the implementation services
  (a standard installation is available), but Meridian requires
  the customisation for operational reasons.

4. ANALYSIS

4.1 Identification of Performance Obligations (IFRS 15.22-30)

A promised good or service is a separate performance obligation
if it is (a) capable of being distinct (IFRS 15.27(a)) and
(b) distinct within the context of the contract (IFRS 15.27(b)).

(a) Perpetual license:
    The software is functional on delivery using a standard
    installation. Meridian could benefit from the license on its
    own or with readily available resources. The license is
    capable of being distinct.

    The implementation services do not significantly modify or
    customise the underlying source code; they configure and
    adapt standard report templates. NovaTech and other vendors
    offer implementation services independently. The license is
    not highly interrelated with or dependent on the
    implementation services within the context of this contract.

    Conclusion: The perpetual license is a separate performance
    obligation.

(b) Implementation and customisation services:
    These services are available from NovaTech and third-party
    consultants independently of the license (several certified
    partners offer equivalent services). They are capable of
    being distinct.

    The implementation does not significantly transform or
    integrate with the license into a combined output -- the
    deliverable is a configured software environment, not a
    fundamentally different product. The services are distinct
    within the context of the contract.

    Conclusion: Implementation/customisation is a separate
    performance obligation.

(c) Post-contract support (PCS):
    PCS (unspecified updates plus technical support) is routinely
    sold separately as a renewal. It is capable of being distinct
    and is separately identifiable in the contract.

    Conclusion: PCS is a separate performance obligation.

    Note: The unspecified updates and the technical support within
    PCS are not separately accounted for because they form a
    single series of services with the same pattern of transfer
    (stand-ready obligation satisfied over time). They are treated
    as one performance obligation.

Summary: Three performance obligations identified.

4.2 Determining the Transaction Price (IFRS 15.47-72)

The total transaction price is CU 2,400,000. There is no
variable consideration, significant financing component, or
non-cash consideration. The transaction price is fixed.

4.3 Allocating the Transaction Price (IFRS 15.73-90)

IFRS 15.76-80 requires allocation based on relative standalone
selling prices (SSP). Observable SSPs exist for all three
performance obligations:

| Performance Obligation | SSP (median) | Relative % | Allocated Price |
|---|---|---|---|
| Perpetual license | CU 1,150,000 | 48.94% | CU 1,174,468 |
| Implementation | CU 600,000 | 25.53% | CU 612,766 |
| PCS (3 years) | CU 600,000 | 25.53% | CU 612,766 |
| **Total** | **CU 2,350,000** | **100.00%** | **CU 2,400,000** |

Calculation: Allocated price = (SSP / Total SSP) x CU 2,400,000

The CU 50,000 premium over the sum of SSPs is distributed
proportionally across all three obligations. No residual
approach or adjusted market assessment is necessary because
observable standalone prices are available.

4.4 Timing of Revenue Recognition (IFRS 15.31-45, B34-B41)

(a) Perpetual license -- point in time or over time?

    Under IFRS 15.B56, a license of intellectual property is
    either a right to access (over time) or a right to use
    (point in time).

    A perpetual license to software version 8.2, where the
    customer is not required to use updated versions and the
    software has significant standalone functionality, represents
    a right to use intellectual property (IFRS 15.B58).

    NovaTech's ongoing activities (PCS updates) do not
    significantly affect the utility of version 8.2 to which
    the customer already has a right. The customer can use
    version 8.2 indefinitely without updates.

    Conclusion: Revenue for the perpetual license is recognised
    at a point in time -- when the customer obtains control,
    i.e., when the license key is delivered and the software is
    available for download/installation. This occurred on
    15 July 2025.

    Revenue recognised: CU 1,174,468 on 15 July 2025.

(b) Implementation services -- point in time or over time?

    IFRS 15.35(a): Meridian simultaneously receives and consumes
    the benefits as NovaTech performs (configuration work enhances
    Meridian's environment progressively). If NovaTech were
    replaced, a new provider would not need to substantially
    re-perform work completed to date.

    Alternatively, under IFRS 15.35(c): NovaTech's performance
    creates an asset (configured environment) that the customer
    controls as it is created.

    Either criterion is met. Implementation revenue is recognised
    over time.

    Measure of progress: input method based on labour hours
    incurred relative to total estimated hours (IFRS 15.41-43).
    This faithfully depicts NovaTech's performance because the
    services involve a relatively homogeneous effort profile.

    At 31 December 2025:
    - Total estimated hours: 2,400
    - Hours incurred to date: 2,160
    - Percentage complete: 90%
    - Revenue recognised to date: CU 612,766 x 90% = CU 551,489
    - Revenue recognised in H2 2025: CU 551,489
    - Remaining to recognise in Q1 2026: CU 61,277

(c) PCS -- point in time or over time?

    PCS is a stand-ready obligation. IFRS 15.35(a) is met:
    the customer simultaneously receives and consumes the
    benefits of NovaTech's stand-ready performance (availability
    of support and updates) as NovaTech performs.

    Revenue is recognised over time. Because the stand-ready
    service is provided uniformly over the support period,
    a time-based measure (straight-line) is appropriate.

    PCS period: 1 July 2025 to 30 June 2028 (3 years).

    Revenue per month: CU 612,766 / 36 months = CU 17,021.

    Revenue recognised in 2025 (6 months): CU 102,128
    Revenue to recognise in 2026 (12 months): CU 204,255
    Revenue to recognise in 2027 (12 months): CU 204,255
    Revenue to recognise in 2028 (6 months): CU 102,128

5. CONCLUSION

   Compliant -- the arrangement contains three distinct
   performance obligations. The proposed treatment is:

   - Perpetual license (CU 1,174,468): point in time on
     delivery (15 July 2025)
   - Implementation (CU 612,766): over time using input
     method (percentage of completion by labour hours)
   - PCS (CU 612,766): over time on a straight-line basis
     over 36 months

   This treatment is consistent with IFRS 15 and supported by
   observable standalone selling prices.

6. JOURNAL ENTRY IMPACT

   (a) 15 July 2025 -- License delivery and first PCS payment

       Dr  Trade receivables          CU 1,200,000
       Dr  Trade receivables          CU 200,000
           Cr  Revenue -- License                     CU 1,174,468
           Cr  Contract liability -- Implementation       CU 24,043
           Cr  Contract liability -- PCS                  CU 97,872
           Cr  Revenue -- PCS (Jul 2025)                 CU 17,021

               pre-paid portion: Aug-Jun)                CU 86,596

       Note: CU 200,000 PCS payment received in advance
       covers Jul 2025 - Jun 2026. Of the CU 1,200,000 license
       payment, CU 1,174,468 is license revenue and CU 25,532
       relates to the implementation obligation (the contractual
       allocation exceeds SSP-based allocation for the license).

       Simplified entries for clarity:

        CU 612,766 - CU 551,489 = CU 61,277.)
```

## 6. Materiality Assessment Template

### Purpose

Document the materiality judgements made in preparing a set of financial statements, so that
both the inclusion of material information and the omission of immaterial information can be
evidenced. IFRS Practice Statement 2 Making Materiality Judgements describes a four-step
process (IFRS Practice Statement 2.33). It is guidance, not a requirement, but it incorporates
the materiality requirements an entity must apply in order to state compliance with IFRS
Accounting Standards (IFRS Practice Statement 2.30).

### The test

Information is material if omitting, misstating or obscuring it could reasonably be expected to
influence decisions that the primary users of general purpose financial statements make on the
basis of those statements. Three things follow that preparers routinely get wrong:

- **Obscuring is a failure, not a neutrality.** Burying material information in an excess of
  immaterial information breaches the requirement just as omission does (IFRS Practice
  Statement 2.30, IFRS Practice Statement 2.57).
- **The cost of complying is not a materiality factor.** An entity may not conclude that a
  disclosure is immaterial because it is expensive to produce, unless a Standard explicitly
  permits that (IFRS Practice Statement 2.37).
- **A uniform percentage threshold is not a materiality policy.** Relying on purely numerical
  guidelines, or applying one threshold across the board, is not appropriate (IFRS Practice
  Statement 2.41).

## 7. Going-Concern Assessment Template

### Purpose

Document management's assessment of the entity's ability to continue as a going concern, and
determine which of four disclosure outcomes applies. The single most common failure in practice
is treating this as binary — going concern or not — and missing the two intermediate outcomes
that carry real disclosure obligations.

### Where the requirement lives

| Annual period beginning | Going-concern assessment and disclosure | Significant judgements | Estimation uncertainty |
|---|---|---|---|
| Before 1 Jan 2027 | IAS 1.25 (requirement), IAS 1.26 (horizon) | IAS 1.122 | IAS 1.125-133 |
| On or after 1 Jan 2027 | IAS 8.6K (requirement), IAS 8.6L (horizon) | IAS 8.27G | IAS 8.31A-31I |

IFRS 18 replaced IAS 1 but did **not** take going concern with it. The requirement moved
unchanged into IAS 8, which was retitled *Basis of Preparation of Financial Statements*.
Both texts are word-for-word identical; only the paragraph address changed.

### Header Fields

| Field | Value |
|---|---|
| **Entity** | [Legal entity name] |
| **Reporting Period** | [e.g., Year ended 31 December 2026] |
| **Reporting date** | [DD/MM/YYYY] |
| **Expected date of authorisation for issue** | [DD/MM/YYYY] |
| **Assessment period used** | [From reporting date to DD/MM/YYYY] |
| **Assessment period length** | [n months from the reporting date] |
| **Prepared by** | [Name, title] |
| **Approved by** | [Board / Audit Committee, date] |

### The horizon

The assessment takes into account all available information about the future, which is **at least,
but is not limited to, twelve months from the end of the reporting period** (IAS 8.6L / IAS 1.26).
Three consequences:

- Twelve months is a **floor, not a cap**. Considering a longer period is not inconsistent with
  the Standard.
- The floor runs from the **reporting date**, not from the date of authorisation. A December
  year end authorised in April has, on the IFRS minimum, looked out only to the following
  December — roughly eight months beyond signing. Many national regulators and lenders require
  twelve months from the *approval* date, and ISA 570 (Revised 2024) requires the auditor to ask
  management to extend its assessment period where that period covers less than twelve months
  from the date of approval of the financial statements.
- The assessment is **dynamic**. It must reflect events up to the date the financial statements
  are authorised for issue (IAS 10.14). If circumstances deteriorate before authorisation such
  that management has no realistic alternative but to cease trading, the financial statements
  must not be prepared on a going concern basis, whatever the position at the reporting date.

|---|---|
| Does the assessment period extend at least 12 months from the reporting date? | [Y/N] |
| Does it extend at least 12 months from the expected authorisation date? | [Y/N — if No, state why, and expect the auditor to request an extension] |
| Have post-reporting-date events up to the authorisation date been reflected? | [Y/N] |
| Is a further update needed before signing? | [Y/N — owner and date] |

### Events and conditions considered

Factors the Standard specifically names are current and expected profitability, debt repayment
schedules and potential sources of replacement financing (IAS 8.6L / IAS 1.26). The degree of
consideration depends on the facts: an entity with a history of profitable operations and ready
access to financial resources may reach its conclusion without detailed analysis.

## 8. Interim Report Checklist (IAS 34)

### Purpose

Verify that an interim financial report meets the minimum content requirements of IAS 34 and
that the significant-events focus has been applied rather than an abbreviated annual report.

### The governing idea

An interim financial report is an **update** on the most recent annual financial statements, not
a smaller version of them. It focuses on new activities, events and circumstances and does not
duplicate information previously reported (IAS 34.6). Users are assumed to have access to the
latest annual report (IAS 34.15A), so relatively insignificant updates to information already in
the annual notes are unnecessary. The corollary is the part that catches preparers out: an
entity may not omit an item merely because it was not in the annual report.

Compliance is all-or-nothing. An interim report may not be described as complying with IFRS
Accounting Standards unless it complies with **all** requirements of IFRS Accounting Standards
(IAS 34.19).

## 10. IFRS 18 Management-defined Performance Measure (MPM) Disclosure Checklist

### Purpose

Identify the entity's management-defined performance measures and build the single MPM note.
This is the requirement that brings "adjusted EBITDA", "underlying operating profit" and
"adjusted EPS" inside the audited financial statements for the first time.

**Effective for annual reporting periods beginning on or after 1 January 2027; earlier
application permitted.** IFRS 18 was issued in April 2024. EU-endorsed 13 February 2026,
published in the Official Journal 16 February 2026.

### Step 1 — Identify the MPMs

A management-defined performance measure is a **subtotal of income and expenses** that:

| Test | Ref | Met? |
|---|---|---|
| (a) is used in public communications outside the financial statements | IFRS 18.117(a) | [ ] |
| (b) communicates management's view of an aspect of the financial performance of the entity **as a whole** | IFRS 18.117(b) | [ ] |
| (c) is not listed in IFRS 18.118 and is not a total or subtotal specifically required to be presented or disclosed by IFRS Accounting Standards | IFRS 18.117(c) | [ ] |

All three must be met.

**A subtotal used in public communications is presumed to be an MPM.** The presumption can be
rebutted only with reasonable and supportable information (IFRS 18.119, IFRS 18.120). Document
any rebuttal.

**Not MPMs — the exhaustive list in IFRS 18.118:**

| Subtotal | Ref |
|---|---|
| Gross profit or loss (revenue minus cost of sales) and similar subtotals | IFRS 18.118(a) |
| Operating profit or loss before depreciation, amortisation and impairments within the scope of IAS 36 | IFRS 18.118(b) |
| Operating profit or loss and income and expenses from all investments accounted for using the equity method | IFRS 18.118(c) |
| For an entity applying IFRS 18.73, a subtotal of operating profit or loss and all income and expenses classified in the investing category | IFRS 18.118(d) |
| Profit or loss before income taxes | IFRS 18.118(e) |
| Profit or loss from continuing operations | IFRS 18.118(f) |

**Also not MPMs:**

| Item | Why | Ref |
|---|---|---|
| A financial ratio | It is not a subtotal of income and expenses. But a subtotal that is the numerator or the denominator of the ratio **is** an MPM if it otherwise meets the definition | IFRS 18.B117 |
| A measure used in the prior period's public communications but with evidence it will not appear in the current period's | Not an MPM this period — but if it was previously disclosed as an MPM, treat it as a cessation under IFRS 18.124 | IFRS 18.B122 |

**Identification working paper**

| 1 | [e.g., Adjusted EBITDA] | | [ ] | [ ] | [ ] | [ ] | [ ] | |

### Step 2 — The single note

| # | Requirement | Ref | Included |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Disclose information about **all** MPMs in a **single note** | IFRS 18.122 | [ ] |
| 2 | Include in that note a statement that the MPMs provide management's view of an aspect of the entity's financial performance as a whole, and are **not necessarily comparable** with measures sharing similar labels or descriptions provided by other entities | IFRS 18.122 | [ ] |
| 3 | Label and describe each MPM in a clear and understandable way that does not mislead users | IFRS 18.123 | [ ] |

The disclosure objective is to enable users to understand (a) the aspect of financial performance

### Step 3 — Per-MPM disclosures (IFRS 18.123)

For **each** MPM:

| # | Requirement | Ref | Included |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | A description of the aspect of financial performance the MPM communicates, **including why, in management's view, it provides useful information about the entity's financial performance** | IFRS 18.123(a) | [ ] |
| 2 | How the MPM is calculated | IFRS 18.123(b) | [ ] |
| 3 | A **reconciliation** between the MPM and the most directly comparable subtotal listed in IFRS 18.118, or a total or subtotal specifically required to be presented or disclosed by IFRS Accounting Standards | IFRS 18.123(c) | [ ] |
| 4 | For **each item** disclosed in the reconciliation: the **income tax effect** and the **effect on non-controlling interests** | IFRS 18.123(d) | [ ] |
| 5 | A description of how the entity determined the income tax effect | IFRS 18.123(e) | [ ] |
| 6 | If the MPM is calculated using anything other than the accounting policies used for items in the statement(s) of financial performance: state that fact and the calculations used | IFRS 18.B134(b)(i) | [ ] |
| 7 | If that calculation also differs from an accounting policy required or permitted by IFRS Accounting Standards: state that fact additionally and, if necessary, explain the meaning of the terms used | IFRS 18.B134(b)(ii) | [ ] |
| 8 | For each reconciling item: the amount(s) relating to **each line item** in the statement(s) of financial performance | IFRS 18.B137(a) | [ ] |
| 9 | For each reconciling item, if necessary: a description of how it is calculated and how it contributes to the MPM providing useful information | IFRS 18.B137(b) | [ ] |

not presented in the statement(s) of financial performance, provided that total or subtotal is
itself reconciled to the most directly comparable presented total or subtotal. The tax and NCI
effects are not required for that additional step (IFRS 18.B140(a), IFRS 18.B140(b)).

**Income tax effect — permitted methods (IFRS 18.B141):**

| Method | Ref |
|---|---|
| Apply the statutory tax rate(s) in the relevant jurisdiction(s) to the underlying transaction(s) | IFRS 18.B141(a) |
| A reasonable pro rata allocation of the current and deferred tax of the entity in the relevant jurisdiction(s) | IFRS 18.B141(b) |
| Another method achieving a more appropriate allocation in the circumstances | IFRS 18.B141(c) |

If more than one method is used, disclose how the tax effect was determined for each reconciling
item (IFRS 18.B142).

### Step 4 — Reconciliation format

Lay the reconciliation out as a **matrix with one column per adjusting item**, not as a list of
adjustments with tax and NCI columns beside them. This is the form the IASB itself illustrates
(IFRS 18 Illustrative Examples, Note 2 — Management-defined performance measures, IE8), and it is
the only layout that satisfies IFRS 18.B137(a) — which requires, for each reconciling item, the
amount relating to **each line item** in the statement(s) of financial performance. A list layout
cannot show that; a matrix does it structurally.

| (in thousands of CU) | IFRS | Impairment losses | Restructuring expenses | Gains on disposal of PP&E | MPM |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Other operating income | | — | — | (1,800) | |
| Research and development expenses | | 1,600 | — | — | |
| General and administrative expenses | | — | 3,800 | — | |
| Goodwill impairment loss | | 4,500 | — | — | |
| **Operating profit / Adjusted operating profit** | **57,000** | **6,100** | **3,800** | **(1,800)** | **65,100** |
| Income tax expense | | — | (589) | 297 | |
| **Profit from continuing operations / Adjusted profit from continuing operations** | **32,100** | **6,100** | **3,211** | **(1,503)** | **39,908** |
| Profit attributable to non-controlling interests | | 305 | 161 | — | |

Read the structure, not the numbers — the amounts are the IASB's illustrative figures and are
there to show the shape. Note what the layout does:

- The **rows above each subtotal are the affected statement of profit or loss line items**, so
  each adjusting item's effect on each line item is visible. That is IFRS 18.B137(a) discharged.
- **Income tax effect and the effect on non-controlling interests are rows, not columns**
  (IFRS 18.123(d)). An impairment that yields no tax deduction shows a genuine nil, not a blank.
- **Two MPMs are reconciled in one table** because they share adjusting items and reconcile to
  two different IFRS subtotals — adjusted operating profit to operating profit, adjusted profit
  from continuing operations to profit from continuing operations. One table, one set of columns.

Below the matrix, provide the narrative required for each adjusting item:

| Adjusting item | Narrative required | Ref |
|---|---|---|
| Each item | How it is calculated, and how it contributes to the MPM providing useful information — where there is more than one reconciling item and each is calculated by a different method or contributes in a different way | IFRS 18.B137(b), IFRS 18.B138 |
| Items sharing a rationale | A single explanation may cover more than one item, or all items collectively — for example one explanation of an entity-specific application of "non-recurring" | IFRS 18.B139 |
| Each item | The income tax effect, with the method used | IFRS 18.123(d), IFRS 18.123(e), IFRS 18.B141 |

Three points that decide whether this note passes review:

- The tax and NCI amounts are required **per reconciling item**, not as a single line at the
  bottom. Most non-GAAP reconciliations published today fail this.
- The reconciliation starts from an IFRS-defined total or subtotal, not from the MPM. Choose the
  **most directly comparable** one (IFRS 18.B136) and be able to say why it is the most directly
  comparable.
- Where an entity applies an entity-specific test such as "non-recurring", the note must say how
  that test is applied, not merely that it is. The IASB's own example spells out which categories
  of litigation expense the entity treats as non-recurring and why (IFRS 18.B139).

Triggered by: a change in how an MPM is calculated; the addition of a new MPM; ceasing to use a
previously disclosed MPM; or a change in how the tax effects of reconciling items are determined.

| # | Requirement | Ref | Included |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | An explanation enabling users to understand the change, addition or cessation and its effects | IFRS 18.124(a) | [ ] |
| 2 | The reasons for the change, addition or cessation | IFRS 18.124(b) | [ ] |
| 3 | Restated comparative information, unless impracticable | IFRS 18.124(c) | [ ] |
| 4 | If comparatives are not restated because it is impracticable: disclose that fact | IFRS 18.125 | [ ] |

### Response quality standards

| Standard | Why |
|---|---|
| Answer the question behind the request | Sending the artefact literally requested, when it does not evidence the assertion, produces a second request and a longer audit |
| Send reconciled, not raw, data | Every schedule submitted should reconcile to the trial balance, and carry the reconciliation |
| Send live models, not PDFs | An estimate model submitted as a flat file cannot be tested and will be requested again |
| One version, version-controlled | Two circulating versions of the same schedule is the most common cause of a repeat request |
| State the judgement, do not bury it | Where the answer rests on a judgement, say so and point to the memo; the auditor will find it regardless and the finding is worse if the entity did not flag it |
| Log every request and every response | The log is the evidence that a request was answered, and it is the input to next year's PBC list |

## Usage Notes

- **Adapt to scope**: These templates can be expanded (add columns, sub-sections) or simplified depending on the engagement.
- **Cross-reference between templates**: A gap analysis (§2) naturally feeds into a disclosure checklist (§1 and §1A) -- use the gaps identified to focus the checklist review. An audit memo (§3) may reference both. The materiality assessment (§6) is what justifies every omission a completed §1A checklist leaves behind, and the audit response template (§11) maps each of these deliverables to the auditor request that calls for it.
- **Which presentation standard applies**: For annual periods beginning before 1 January 2027, IAS 1 governs presentation and disclosure. From 1 January 2027 (or earlier on early adoption) IFRS 18 replaces it, and going concern, the fair presentation override, material accounting policy information, judgements and estimation uncertainty move into IAS 8, retitled *Basis of Preparation of Financial Statements*. §1A carries the row-by-row mapping; §7 and §5 carry the dual references inline. A checklist citing only IAS 1 will be wrong for an IFRS 18 adopter, and one citing only IAS 8 will be wrong for everyone reporting now.
- **Version control**: Include a version history table at the top of each completed deliverable when used on live engagements.
- **Professional judgment**: Templates structure the analysis but do not replace judgment. All conclusions should be reviewed by a qualified professional.
