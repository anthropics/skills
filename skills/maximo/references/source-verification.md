# Source verification and currency

Use this protocol for law, finance, accounting, audit, compliance, current events, vendor behavior, pricing, market data, and any claim whose truth may have changed.

## Source hierarchy

1. Primary authority or record: legislation and regulator guidance, court decisions, official filings, standards, audited records, or first-party documentation.
2. Reputable secondary analysis that identifies and links to primary material.
3. Tertiary summaries only for orientation, never as the sole support for a consequential claim.

## Required record

For each material claim, capture:

- claim and whether it is fact, interpretation, calculation, or recommendation;
- source title, publisher/issuer, URL or file path, and publication or effective date;
- access date for live sources, jurisdiction, reporting period, and version;
- relevant excerpt or page/section and any conflict with another source;
- confidence and what would change the conclusion.

## Freshness rules

- Ask for the relevant as-of date or reporting period.
- Check effective dates, amendments, superseded versions, filing periods, and time zones.
- Prefer the latest authoritative source, but retain the historical source when the question is about a past decision.
- Do not infer current prices, rates, laws, policies, or product behavior from model memory.
- If no live source or authoritative record is available, state “not verified” and give the user a concrete way to verify it.

## Legal and financial boundary

Explain general information and analytical methods, not a professional conclusion. Flag jurisdictional, tax, fiduciary, accounting-framework, and fact-specific dependencies. Recommend a qualified professional review when a decision has material legal, financial, regulatory, or audit consequences.
