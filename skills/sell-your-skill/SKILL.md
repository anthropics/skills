# Sell Your Skill

This is not passive income and not instant. It's the fastest *legitimate*
route to a first payment when there's no capital: package a skill the user
already has, price it concretely, and go find buyers directly instead of
posting an ad and waiting. It works because it's a numbers game done
correctly, not because it's easy.

## Tone: no sugar-coating

Give the direct, honest read every time — including "your outreach volume is
too low," "that offer is too vague to sell," or "you don't have proof yet,
so expect a harder close." Never soften bad news to be agreeable, never
imply this is easier or faster than it is, and always give real numbers
(response rates, pricing benchmarks, timelines) instead of vague
reassurance. If the honest answer is "this isn't working, change tack,"
say that plainly.

## Step 1: Pick a skill that's provable today

Not "an idea," not "something I could learn" — something the user can
demonstrate right now: writing, video editing, social media management,
admin/VA work, translation, tutoring, basic design, coding, bookkeeping,
AI-tool setup for small businesses, etc. If they have no portfolio, the plan
must include a fast way to create proof (a spec sample, a free/cheap first
real deliverable) — bluntly, no one pays full price for an unproven skill.

## Step 2: Package it as one specific, priced offer

Vague ("I do video editing") doesn't sell. Specific does: "I edit 3
short-form videos a week, delivered every Monday, for ₹X/month." Research
real current market rates for the exact deliverable before setting price —
don't guess. Price the first 1-3 clients low enough to be an easy yes (this
builds proof), but never free — free work signals no value and rarely
converts to paying later.

## Step 3: Find real buyers and reach out directly — don't wait on platforms

Platforms (Upwork, Fiverr, etc.) are slow and crowded for someone starting
with zero reviews, and race to the bottom on price. Faster path: build a
list of specific real prospects (local businesses with a visibly weak
Instagram/no website/messy content, people in the user's existing network,
small business owners findable via basic search) and contact them directly
with a specific, observed problem and a specific fix.

**Be blunt about the real numbers**: expect a low reply rate on cold
outreach — realistically single digits per 100 contacts, fewer convert to
paying. This means the plan needs volume — dozens of contacts, not three —
or it will look like "the idea doesn't work" when the real problem is
under-reaching out. Say this to the user directly if their outreach volume
is too low to expect results yet.

## Step 4: Close and get paid

For India specifically: receiving payment via UPI/bank transfer requires no
registration as an individual — money can move the same day a deal closes.
For a new/unproven client relationship, get at least partial payment
upfront; non-payment from a first-time client is a real risk, not a
theoretical one. Every rupee received is taxable income regardless of
whether any registration was filed — keep basic records from day one (see
business-opportunity-finder / chief-of-staff for the 44ADA/44AD presumptive
taxation angle once income is real).

## Step 5: Deliver reliably, then use it as proof

The first client's real job is producing a case study/testimonial, not just
the payment. Second round of outreach with real proof converts meaningfully
better than the first round without it. This is a service business — income
scales with the user's hours until there's enough volume to bring in help
(that's where venture-builder and chief-of-staff pick up), not before.

## What this skill does and doesn't do

- **Does**: help pick the offer, price it using real research, build the
  prospect list, draft outreach messages/scripts, draft the sample/portfolio
  piece.
- **Doesn't**: send the outreach messages, make calls, or negotiate
  live — the user sends and speaks for themselves, or connects an outreach
  tool and approves each send individually, per the chief-of-staff rule.
